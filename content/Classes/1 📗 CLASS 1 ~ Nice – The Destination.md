### FULL SCRIPT – CLASS #1

The first “Class” in this “Course” is what I refer to as “Nice.”

Nice is admittedly a rather vague and nebulous term, but it nicely describes a state that we should continuously strive to reach — as individuals, as groups of any size or shape, as nations, and as a species.

If we use the “destination” analogy for our journey through life, then “Nice” is where we’re driving to. Reaching “Nice” should be our overarching focus.

It’s also my opinion that “Nice” must become the overriding, sole objective that we train artificial intelligence to pursue. AI’s governor and ultimate goal must be “Nice” — otherwise, it will become the largest contributor to our downfall as a species.

I would go so far as to say that if AI strays even one iota from “Nice” as its strict guiding principle, it will either cause or heavily contribute to humanity’s demise.

This, I think, is a fact.

So what is “Nice”? How is it defined?

The definition of “Nice” is very simple. I define “Nice” in four (4) ways:

---

### Chapter 1.1: It’s Best to Think of Others Not Just Yourself

The first definition of “Nice” is: “It’s best to think of others, not just yourself — and that includes the natural world.”

This does not mean you should never think of yourself. If you don’t take care of yourself, you’ll fail in ways that prevent you from achieving other goals. But the point is, thinking of others as well sets up a win-win-win.

It helps others, and it makes you feel better because we are social animals.

An accompanying idea here is that peace of mind, unselfishness, warmheartedness, compassion, kindness, and social interaction tend to make humans the happiest.

These simple ideas should be humanity’s goal — and AI’s as well — if we want long-term alignment with our best interests.

AI needs to be trained to see the wisdom in these concepts and accept them as its strict governor and sole ultimate objective.

---

### Chapter 1.2: The Harvard Study on Happiness

The second definition of “Nice” draws from the Harvard Study on Happiness.

This long-term study shows that humans are social animals, and what makes us happiest is meaningful relationships and positive social interaction.

### Chapter 1.3: It’s Nicer to Be Nice

The third definition comes from my mother, who always said: "It’s nicer to be nice."

And if you think about it — it really is.

Unless you’re dealing with a malignant narcissist, sociopath, or psychopath — it’s almost always nicer to be nice.

Have you noticed? Niceness tends to beget more niceness. Nastiness tends to beget more nastiness. This seems to be a universal human truth.

So, as my wise and lovely mother said: "It’s nicer to be nice."

---

### Chapter 1.4: Bodett’s 3 Ingredients to Happiness

The fourth definition of “Nice” is something someone once attributed to Tom Bodett (though I haven’t confirmed it):

To be truly happy, you only need three things:

- Someone to love  
      
    
- Something to do  
      
    
- Something to hope for  
      
    

This concept applies not just to individuals but also to things like nationalism.

There’s nothing wrong with being proud of your group, your tribe, or your nation — if that pride is used for good. It should not be used to divide.

---

### Conclusion

If you combine all four definitions of “Nice,” it’s hard to argue against them. They create a powerful, meaningful direction to strive for.

“Nice” must be our desired destination. It’s what both humans and AI need to continuously pursue.

Some say striving for “Nice” is a waste of time or inane tripe — but they’re wrong.

Setting ideals is not a weakness. It’s important. And for humanity, it may prove to be essential.

Using the road trip analogy: you must determine your destination before you can plan a path. And if AI is going to help us — not destroy us — then “Nice” must be its strict governor and sole focus.
